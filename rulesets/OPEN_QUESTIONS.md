# Open questions on the rules of «Studiolo» (placeholders)

A placeholder is a value that stands in a game file so that the game can be played, and waits to be replaced. It is either our decision where the sources are silent (mark [S]), or our reading where the sources disagree (mark [R]).

How a placeholder is replaced:
- **document** — a source has been found where the rule is printed;
- **expert** — the opinion of a specialist in the reconstruction of rules, with a reference to the argument;
- **math** — a mathematical analysis;
- **bots** — statistics of games played by bots and of the server logs;
- **people** — a trial with live players.

Replacing a placeholder is a new revision of the rules and an entry in `REVISIONS.md`. A question is not deleted from this list: it is given the status «closed» and the number of the revision.

**Settled by the project.** On 26 November 2026 the project settled sixteen questions on which no new source is expected soon: six questions of taste (6, 13, 18, 23, 24, 26) and ten small readings (12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 19, 27, 28, 30, 31). What stands now is the rule of «Studiolo». No game file changed, so this is not a new revision. A settled question is not worked on any further; it is reopened only by a new input: a document, an expert's argument, statistics of games, or what players tell us. The marks [S] and [R] and the field `replace` stay in the game files: they still say whose decision the rule is and what could replace it. Questions 17 and 30 are settled as questions: the text is in hand, and what is left is a task for the core.

**When a game is finished.** On 27 November 2026 the project fixed the rule by which it counts a game as finished. A game of the Studiolo is finished when four things hold: the engine plays what the file says and the score is right; every rule carries a source and a mark; where the sources allow two readings, the second stands beside the one that is played; and an outside reviewer has played the game through. After revision 14 all 21 games count as finished. A finished rule changes on three grounds: a new document, an argument from a specialist, or a defect that a live player ran into. We do not start new rounds of checking on our own. A case that happens less than once in 10,000 deals and does not break the table is written down and left as it is. We do not mend the balance of a historical game: we rebuild the game as it was played, we do not improve it.

In the game files a placeholder is marked with the field `replace` carrying the number of the question; the other version, if it can be written as a value, stands in the field `alt`. The list by files is built by the command `npm run open-questions`. Questions 3, 13 and 17 are not marked with `replace` in the files: their rule is not played yet (since revision 8 question 17 is named in the text of the Bagatto payments of Mitigati for two and of the Bagatto declared last in Bagatt'ultimo and Permesso for three to five).

Pages are those of the printed books. «Rules of 1830» is «Regole inalterabili», Torino, 1830 (a copy in Oxford, Jessel f.937; we do not hold it). «Torino [1846]» is L. B., «Il giuocatore di tarocchi al tavoliere», part I; «Prammatica» is part II of the same book; it was also issued as a separate booklet (cover dated 1846, deposited 27 December 1845), printed from the same setting with the same pages. The two parts are paged separately, so a page of part I is always written «Torino [1846], part I, p. …». «Annecy» is the manuscript rules from Annecy of the late 18th century, published by Depaulis in 1984.

«Census» is a count of rare cases over 10,000 deals of random robots (an engine run, 23 November 2026). The robots play at random: for people, everything that depends on skill will be rarer.
